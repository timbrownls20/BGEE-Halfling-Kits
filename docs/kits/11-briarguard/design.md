# Briarguard

*Some halflings guard the hedgerows rather than tend them. They wear what the wild gives (hide, chitin, dragon scale), carry a stout club, and fight with the badger at their heel and the thorns at their back. Fire and frost wash off them like rain off a holly leaf.*

**Base class:** Fighter. **Race:** Halfling only. **Requires:** Wisdom 13 or higher.

A halfling-only fighter kit for Baldur's Gate 1 and 2: a nature warrior blessed by Sheela Peryroyl. It gives up metal armour and martial weapons for innate nature spells, elemental resistance that grows to 80%, and a badger familiar. Target tier: rated 8 overall, a hard-to-kill front-liner whose defences come from nature rather than plate.

## Advantages

- **Weapon skill:** can reach grandmastery in every weapon it can use: clubs, quarterstaffs, slings and short bows, all of wood, leather and stone. In the Enhanced Editions grandmastery's extra attack works with slings and bows too.
- **Small and Nimble:** +2 AC from level 1.
- **Heartwood Hide:** resistance to fire, cold, electricity and acid (including magical fire and cold) that grows with level: 10% at 1, 25% at 5, 40% at 10, 60% at 15, and 80% at 20.
- **Wild Stride:** from level 1, immune to entangle, web and Spike Growth, so it can fight inside its own Entangle. From level 12 it is also immune to hold, paralysis and slow. Unlike the game's Free Action, it doesn't block speed bonuses, so Haste and Boots of Speed still work.
- **Innate nature abilities:** Entangle, Barkskin, Strength of the Oak, Iron Skins and Wake the Hedge (a plant guardian that grows with it), gained as it levels (details below).
- **Find Familiar:** from level 1, it can call a badger familiar once as an innate ability (details below).

## Disadvantages

- **Natural armour only:** no metal armour. It can wear leather, hide, Ankheg Plate and the dragon-scale armours, but not studded leather, chain, splint or plate.
- **Natural weapons only: clubs, quarterstaffs, slings and short bows. No metal weapons (swords, axes, spears, flails, maces, hammers, daggers), and no** longbows or crossbows.
- **Shields:** bucklers and small shields only. Most are metal, so this is the one place the natural theme bends, which saves flagging every shield by material.
- Must be a neutral good or true neutral halfling.

**Strength of the Oak:** for 1 turn, +2 Strength and +2 Constitution. It lifts a halfling past its Strength cap of 17, so it is the Briarguard's damage boost for hard fights. The Deep Roots HLA adds regeneration to it.

**Wake the Hedge:** the hedgerow itself stands up and fights for 2 turns: one tough plant guardian that holds the line beside the Briarguard. Its blows entangle what they hit (save vs breath), and like all wood it takes extra damage from fire.

The guardian is a real creature of the wild answering Sheela's call, so Death Spell can't kill it. It counts towards the limit of 5. Iron Skins on a fighter is the kit's main defence once metal armour is out of reach.

## Familiar

**Find Familiar:** from level 1, the Briarguard can call a familiar once as an innate ability. It is always a badger, whatever the Briarguard's alignment, and it grows with the Briarguard. Like any familiar, it adds to its master's hit points and can be carried in the pack, where it heals. It isn't a summon, so it doesn't count towards the limit of 5. If it dies, the Briarguard loses the bonus hit points and 1 Constitution until it next rests, and can call the badger again after resting.

Where the Greenhand's rabbit is a scout, the badger is a scrapper: it fights beside its master, and its abilities guard the front line. Each form keeps everything from the forms before it.

## Balance notes

The Briarguard trades plate for nature's defences. Early on it is a little softer than a plate-wearing fighter, but Barkskin from level 1 and Ankheg Plate cover most of the gap. From level 10, Iron Skins does what plate can't, and its elemental resistance makes it the best answer in the halfling kits to dragon breath, Fireballs and Cone of Cold, rising to 80% at 20 (deliberately short of immunity, since Iron Skins already covers physical damage). Its damage is solid rather than high: grandmastery in every weapon (including the extra attack with slings and short bows) makes up for small damage dice, and Strength of the Oak lifts its Strength past the halfling cap of 17 when it matters.

It overlaps with the Surefoot as a front-liner: the Surefoot is the harder tank against physical damage and disabling effects, the Briarguard against elements, and it brings a plant guardian, Entangle and, in ToB, Call the Pack. The Greenhand is the summoner; the Briarguard is the warrior who brings a little of the wild with it. It has no healing of its own beyond potions and Deep Roots.

Its weakness is magic, and that is deliberate: it has no magic resistance and no protection from charm, confusion or domination, and a charmed grandmaster is dangerous to its own party. Resist Magic is on its HLA list, and in a party the Unbeguiled's Clear Heads covers the gap.

**Rough ratings:** Damage 7.5, Survivability 8.5, Party value 6.5, Overall 8, Theme 10, Solo 7.5.

## Implementation notes

For anyone building this kit as a mod (for example with WeiDU):

- **Easy:** weapon limits, Small and Nimble, Heartwood Hide (elemental resistance granted by level through the kit's ability table), Entangle, Barkskin and Iron Skins (existing druid spells granted as innate abilities), Strength of the Oak, Deep Roots, Heartwood, Briar Patch and Call the Pack (existing entangle effects and creatures), Ironwood (the weapon enchantment level the kit counts as), Badger's Fury, Thornhide and Hedge Shelter (standard buff and damage-shield effects).
- **Moderate:** natural-only armour (each allowed or forbidden armour item has to be flagged for the kit, since the game doesn't track what armour is made of), Wild Stride (separate immunities to hold, paralysis, slow, entangle, web and the specific slowing spells, rather than the game's Free Action effect, so speed bonuses still work), Wake the Hedge (three new plant creatures, though the Young Treant can reuse the game's treant), and Find Familiar (a custom innate copy of the spell without the main-character limit, and five badger creature files that swap as the Briarguard levels).

### As built (not yet tested in game)

Code: `mod/halflingkits/kits/11-briarguard/`. Where the build differs from the design, or relies on something to check in game:

- **Find Familiar is not built yet**, and neither is the badger's part of Badger's Fury. A true familiar needs a script (following the party, riding in the pack, adding hit points to its master, and costing Constitution when it dies). This is scriptable, not EEex, and is planned as its own pass.
- **Natural armour** is decided by item name at install: names containing leather, hide, Ankheg or dragon are allowed, unless they say studded; all other armour is blocked. `hkbgarm.2da` in the kit folder corrects any item the names get wrong (one row per item: resref, then ALLOW or DENY). This answers open question 3 in the Handover with a rule; the item-by-item check still needs a game install.
- **Entangle, Barkskin and Iron Skins** are copies of the game's own spells, found by name at install.
- **Wild Stride** blocks the entangle and web effects and the game's own Entangle and Spike Growth spells, and sets a spell state that the kit's own entangles (Briar Patch and the plant guardians' blows) check, so it is never caught in them.
- **Entangled** (Briar Patch, plant guardians) is the entangle overlay plus movement stopped, with one save vs breath. Briar Patch's piercing damage hits everyone in the area, allies and the Briarguard included; only the entangle spares the Briarguard.
- **Plant guardians** are copies of the game's Shambling Mound (Bramble Man, Thornwood Guardian) and Treant (Young Treant) with the design's hit points and AC, on-hit entangle, 50% weakness to fire and immunity to Death Spell. Their attacks keep the source creature's damage dice rather than the design's 1d10, 2d8 and 3d6.
- **Call the Pack** copies the game's Cave Bear and Dire Wolf. The +2 to hit and fear immunity last the full turn, even if the pack falls sooner.
- **Hedge Shelter** gives +3 AC (standing in for "Barkskin's AC bonus") and reuses Bless's party-only area, not exactly 15 feet.
- **Thornhide** fires on any hit, ranged as well as melee.
- **Ironwood** uses the engine's weapon enchantment bonus on both hands; check in game that it lets the Briarguard hit creatures that need +3 and +4 weapons.
- **Strength of the Oak** losing its +2 Constitution when it ends is open question 4 in the Handover; not changed.
