# Ghostwise Rogue

*The Ghostwise keep to the deep Chondalwood, far from roads and towns. They speak mind to mind without a word, ride giant owls, and paint their faces before a hunt. The Ghostwise Rogue is what that life makes of a halfling: silent in the trees, savage in a fight, and quite unable to see the point of a locked door.*

**Base class:** Thief. **Race:** Halfling only. **Requires:** Constitution 14 or higher.

A halfling-only thief kit for Baldur's Gate 1 and 2: a wild hunter with a thief's stealth, a barbarian's rage, an owl familiar and a family amulet. It gives up the town thief's skills (no Open Locks or Pick Pockets), its magic items and its tricks with scrolls and potions. Target tier: rated 8 overall, the halfling ranger: a scout and skirmisher who rages when caught, rather than an assassin.

The placeholder called for a fighter/thief multiclass. The game doesn't support kits on multiclass characters without an extra mod, so it was rebuilt as a single-class thief with barbarian qualities (decided 16 October 2026): no dependency, and it works on every platform. A later trim (also 16 October) cut the barbarian's toughness (Thick Hide, Wild Hide, Fleet of Foot) and Hunter's Pace, leaving rage as its one barbarian trait.

## Advantages

- **Rage:** 1 use per day at level 1, plus 1 more every 4 levels (5, 9, 13 and so on), as the Barbarian. For 5 rounds: +4 Strength, +4 Constitution, +2 to saves vs spells and -2 AC, and immune to charm, hold, fear, maze, stun, sleep, confusion and level drain. There is no fatigue when it ends.
- **Ambush:** an innate ability with the same uses as Rage. Used while hidden, for 1 round the Rogue's hits (melee or missile) pin their target in place: it can't move and has -2 AC for 2 rounds (3 from level 13). It can still fight and cast. A save vs paralysis avoids being held in place but not the AC penalty. It adds no damage: the Ghostwise hunt by pinning prey for the pack, not by a knife in the back. Pinned from range, a target can neither close on the Rogue nor flee.
- **Hunter's Eye:** +1 to hit at level 1, plus 1 more every 4 levels, up to +6 at level 21. It closes half the gap between a thief's THAC0 and a fighter's, and applies to every attack.
- **Small and Nimble:** +2 AC.
- **Child of the Wood:** outdoors and away from towns, +1 damage and +15% to Hide in Shadows and Move Silently. Indoors, in towns and in dungeons it is an ordinary thief.
- **Weapon skill:** can reach specialisation (2 pips) in spears, axes, short swords, slings and short bows, and proficiency in its other weapons (clubs and daggers). Can put 3 pips (mastery) in Two-Weapon Style.
- **Thief skills:** Hide in Shadows, Move Silently, Find Traps, Set Traps and Detect Illusion, with 15 points per level to spread over five skills (as the Assassin) rather than the usual 25 over seven, so losing the town skills is a real cost.
- **Find Familiar:** from level 1, it can call an owl once per day as an innate ability (see below).
- **Family Amulet:** see below.

## Disadvantages

- **No town skills:** cannot Open Locks or Pick Pockets.
- **No backstab:** a hunter, not an assassin. Attacking from hiding does no extra damage (as the Swashbuckler); Ambush takes its place.
- **Hunter's weapons only:** short swords, spears, clubs, axes, daggers, slings and short bows.
- **Armour:** leather and studded leather only: no Elven Chain or mithril, which other thieves rely on later. Bucklers only.
- **No Use Any Item, Scribe Scrolls or Alchemy:** the Ghostwise make nothing from paper or glass, and trust nothing they didn't make.
- **Ancestors' grudge:** -1 Luck from the Family Amulet, at every level.
- **No other amulets:** the Family Amulet fills the amulet slot for good.
- Requires Constitution 14 or higher.
- Must be a neutral good, chaotic good or chaotic neutral halfling.

## The family amulet

Every Ghostwise child is given the clan's amulet, a disc of owl bone carved with the names of its ancestors. It can't be taken off, and it grows as the Rogue does. The ancestors guard their own, but they also bring their old feuds with them: the wearer always has -1 Luck.

## The owl familiar

The Ghostwise bond with owls. The Rogue's owl is a scout that sees what hides and harries what runs, rather than a fighter. It grows as the Rogue levels. While it lives the Rogue gains bonus hit points. Each form keeps everything from the forms before it.

Like the Briarguard's badger and the Greenhand's rabbit, it is a true familiar when the main character calls it: it follows between areas, rides in the pack, and its death costs 1 Constitution until the next rest. Called by anyone else, it is a summon for one day, and its death costs no Constitution.

## Balance notes

The Ghostwise Rogue is the halfling ranger: halflings can't be rangers in this game, and this kit fills that gap with a wilderness hunter, an animal companion and stealth, on a thief's chassis. It is a scout and skirmisher: it hides, pins a target with Ambush (from range if it likes), and rages when the fight comes to it. Hunter's Eye closes half the THAC0 gap, and rage, its owl's bonus hit points and the Family Amulet's saves and magic resistance keep it standing longer than other thieves, but it has no extra attacks and no physical resistance, so it isn't a front-liner. It pays with a d6 hit die, no Open Locks or Pick Pockets and only 15 skill points a level, no backstab, leather and studded leather with no Elven Chain or mithril, bucklers only, no Use Any Item, -1 Luck and no amulet but its own. Outdoors, Child of the Wood makes it a strong skirmisher; in cities and dungeons it is noticeably weaker, which matters for The Wild Wood party in BG2's dungeons. Its owl gives the party a second set of eyes (invisibility, then True Seeing), which matters for a party like The Wild Wood that otherwise relies on the Greenhand for divination.

Against the Fleet Foot, the Fleet Foot is the melee duellist and the Ghostwise the scout who pins targets. Against the Briarguard (both nature kits with a familiar), the Briarguard owns physical and elemental toughness; the Ghostwise has rage, stealth and magic resistance from its amulet. Against the Slinger, the Slinger is the ranged damage dealer; the Ghostwise's missiles set up kills rather than make them.

**Rough ratings:** Damage 6, Survivability 7, Party value 7, Overall 8, Theme 9, Solo 7.

## Implementation notes

For anyone building this kit as a mod (for example with WeiDU):

- **Easy:** Rage (an innate copy of the Barbarian's rage, uses every 4 levels in the CLAB), Hunter's Eye, Small and Nimble, no backstab (a x1 row in `BACKSTAB.2DA`, as the Swashbuckler), Ambush (an innate that gives the Rogue melee and missile hit effects, opcodes 248 and 249, for 1 round; the pin is movement set to 0, opcode 176 — check in game whether Free Action blocks it), Wild Rage (a spell state the rage checks, as the Surefoot's stances), Owl's Mark, Silent Speech, Vanish into the Wood, Call the Parliament, the armour and shield limits (base AC 7 or more, which also blocks Elven Chain and mithril; bucklers only).
- **Moderate:**
  - **Ambush only while hidden:** the innate should refuse to work unless the Rogue is hidden. Find a `SPLPROT.2DA` row (or add one) that tests the invisible state, for opcode 318 to block the ability otherwise. If none works, it is usable at any time, and its uses keep it in check.
  - **Family Amulet:** five items (one per step), each with its saves, magic resistance and -1 Luck as equipped effects, flagged cursed and not droppable. A CLAB spell at level 1 puts the first in the amulet slot (opcode 143); at 7, 12, 18 and 24 a CLAB spell removes the old one (opcode 112) and puts the next in its place. Check in game: what opcode 143 does when the slot already holds an amulet (an imported character), and whether Remove Curse lets the amulet be taken off; if it does, the next level step puts it back, or a rest script can.
  - **Spears and axes:** a thief can't normally use them. They need opening to this kit only, as the Magpie's scrolls and wands were: clear the "unusable by thief" flag, then block every other thief kit.
  - **No Open Locks or Pick Pockets:** lower both skills out of reach, as the Magpie's Set Traps. **15 skill points per level:** a row in `THIEFSKL.2DA`, as the Magpie's.
  - **Child of the Wood:** a global-script check per party slot that applies the outdoor bonus when the area is outdoors and not a city, and removes it otherwise (like the True Heart's fall check).
  - **The owl familiar:** the badger's and rabbit's approach (`HK_FAMILIAR`): a familiar for the main character, a one-day summon for anyone else. It needs an owl or bird creature to copy; if the game has none, another small creature stands in.
- **Hard:** none.
