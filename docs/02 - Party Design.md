# Party Design

How the eleven halfling kits fit together into all-halfling parties: what each kit brings, six line-ups (two of six, two of four, a trio and a duo), two guest line-ups with an elf Sorcerer (Snow White and the Five Shorties, and the Three Shorties), and the swaps between them. Ratings for each kit are on the Overview and Strength Rankings tabs.

## What each kit brings

| Kit | Best role | Brings to a party | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fleet Foot Fighter | Melee striker | The highest damage of any kit; races past the front line to cut down casters and archers; Time Slow and Time Pause in ToB | Light armour; roams rather than holding the line |
| Surefoot Fighter | Main tank | Near-complete immunities, Hold the Line, and Still Ground to shut down nearby casters | Still Ground stops the party's own casting nearby too; low damage |
| True Heart | Tank and healer | Heart immunities, Lay on Hands, Cleansing Touch, Aura of Courage and the Heart healing chain | The main healer stands on the front line |
| Tymora's Champion | Second tank or melee fighter | Luck-based AC and saves, Lucky Break and Second Chance | Its luck only helps itself |
| Unbeguiled | Anti-mage front-liner | Magic resistance, Clear Heads (allies immune to charm, confusion and domination), Spoiling Strike Breaches protected mages | No magic rings, amulets or cloaks; Lore 0; modest damage |
| Slinger | Ranged damage | Up to 5 sling attacks a round; Breaching and Dispelling bullets for protected mages | Fragile if caught |
| Hearthsinger | Singing priest | Hearthsong, plate, cleric healing and buffs; innate Heal, Iron Skins and Resurrection HLAs in ToB | Little damage of its own |
| Greenhand | Nature priest | Healing, summons that Death Spell can't kill, nature area spells (Insect Plague, Creeping Doom), Negative Plane Protection, True Seeing, a rabbit familiar | Studded leather; no Chaotic Commands, Holy Word or Turn Undead |
| Briarguard | Elemental tank | Elemental resistance up to 80%, Iron Skins, immunity to hold and slow, Entangle, a plant guardian that grows with it, a badger familiar; grandmastery in clubs, staves, slings and short bows | No metal armour; no healing of its own |
| Magpie | Thief and stand-in mage | Locks, traps, scouting, and arcane scrolls and wands through Magpie's Knack | Scrolls can fumble early; arcane magic is only as good as its stock |
| Nettle | Anti-mage thief | Spoiling Backstab and Spoiling Traps take apart protected casters; Non-Detection keeps it hidden | No backstab multiplier; small weapons; Lore 0 |

**No kit is a mage.** Every halfling party has to answer enemy magic without Haste, Stoneskin, Spell Sequencers or Time Stop, so the real design question is which mix of anti-magic tools (the Unbeguiled, the Slinger's bullets, the Nettle, the Magpie's scrolls, the priests' Dispel Magic and True Seeing) a party carries.

## Six-person parties

### Recommended six: the anti-magic party

| Position | Kit | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main tank | True Heart | Very hard to kill and a second source of healing; unlike the Surefoot, it doesn't stop the priests casting |
| Off-tank, anti-mage | Unbeguiled | Clear Heads protects the party's minds; Spoiling Strike Breaches protected mages; its magic resistance soaks hostile spells |
| Melee damage | Fleet Foot Fighter | The highest damage of all the kits, and fast enough to reach enemy casters |
| Ranged damage | Slinger | Strong ranged damage, plus Breaching and Dispelling bullets in ToB |
| Priest | Greenhand | Area damage, summons that soak hits, Negative Plane Protection, True Seeing and healing |
| Thief | Magpie | Locks, traps, scouting, and wands and scrolls in place of a mage |

This is the strongest six the kits make. It answers the original six's weak spot, enemy magic, three ways: the Unbeguiled's magic resistance and Clear Heads, the Greenhand's area spells (the original six has almost none), and a field full of summons Death Spell can't wipe out. The Greenhand's missing Chaotic Commands doesn't matter here, because Clear Heads covers the same ground.

**How it plays:** the True Heart and Unbeguiled hold the front, with the Greenhand's summons widening the line. The Slinger strips protections from enemy casters, the Unbeguiled spoils the ones that close in, and the Fleet Foot goes round the side to finish them. The Greenhand opens big fights with Insect Plague or Creeping Doom and backs up the True Heart's healing; the Magpie scouts ahead and fills gaps with scrolls.

**What it gives up:** physical toughness at the front (the Unbeguiled rates 7.5 for Survivability against the Surefoot's 10, and can't wear magic rings or cloaks, so the True Heart and the summons carry more of the load); the Hearthsinger's songs, including Homeward Hymn's extra attack on the Fleet Foot and Slinger; and, as with every halfling party, a real mage.

**Difficulty: about standard.** Easier than the original six against mages and mind control, slightly harder in long physical slugfests.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BG1 (levels 1–8) | Fleet Foot and Slinger kill quickly. The Greenhand's Summon Horde rabbits draw blows and its berries and Harvest Loaf keep everyone going. The Unbeguiled is immune to charm from level 1 and makes enemy casters fumble spells. The Magpie handles locks and traps. | Low HP all round. Poison until the True Heart's immunity at 5 (the Greenhand's berries cure it from 5 too). Magpie scrolls fumble early. | Easy |
| Early SoA (9–14) | Iron Skins and Insect Plague arrive. Clear Heads makes nearby allies immune to charm from 8, and Spoiling Strike Breaches from 12. Negative Plane Protection covers vampires. The True Heart gets charm and sleep immunity at 10. | Clear Heads covers only charm until 16, so confusion and domination still land on the party. Spoiling Strike has to hit to work. | Moderate |
| Late SoA (15–20) | Heal from 11, Creeping Doom, Greater Restoration and Resurrection from 14. Clear Heads adds confusion at 16. Elementals and Animal Summoning fill the field. | Still no Haste or Stoneskin. Dragon breath and big area spells hit the lightly armoured back line hard. The front line is thinner than the Surefoot's. | Moderate |
| Throne of Bhaal (21+) | Clear Heads adds domination at 24. Breaching and Dispelling bullets, the Unbeguiled's Stand Behind Me and Disenchanting Strike, the Greenhand's Hedge Warden, Call the Bog and Storm of Vengeance, the True Heart's Undying Heart, the Fleet Foot's Time Slow and Time Pause, and the Magpie's 8th and 9th-level scrolls. | No Time Stop. Heavily layered liches still take careful sequencing. | Comfortable |

### Original six: two tanks, two damage dealers, two supports

| Pair | Kit | Role in the pair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tanks | Surefoot Fighter | Main tank: anchors the front with Hold the Line; Still Ground shuts down nearby casters, but it stops the party's own casting nearby too, so the True Heart and Hearthsinger should stand clear while it is up |
| Tanks | True Heart | Off-tank and healer: plate, Heart immunities, Aura of Courage, Cleansing Touch and the Heart healing chain, all strongest from the front line |
| Damage | Fleet Foot Fighter | Melee striker: races past the front line to cut down mages, priests and archers |
| Damage | Slinger | Ranged damage; Breaching and Dispelling Shot strip mage protections |
| Support | Hearthsinger | Hearthsong and cleric spells to 5th level (including True Seeing and Raise Dead) |
| Support | Magpie | Locks, traps and scouting; arcane scrolls and wands through Magpie's Knack |

Each pair covers the other's gaps: the Fleet Foot reaches targets the Slinger cannot, the Slinger strips protections the Fleet Foot cannot, and the Magpie fills the arcane gap the Hearthsinger leaves. It is the toughest six physically and the best for songs, but the weakest against magic.

**How it plays:** the Surefoot and True Heart form a two-wide front line that enemies struggle to slip past, with the True Heart's auras and healing covering both; the Slinger strips protections from enemy casters; the Fleet Foot goes round the side to finish them; the Hearthsinger sings and backs up the healing; the Magpie scouts ahead, opens the way and fills gaps with scrolls. Because the main healer is also on the front line, keep Undying Heart and the Hearthsinger's raising spells in reserve in case the True Heart goes down.

**Difficulty: a little harder than a standard party.** BG1 and early SoA are comfortable, since the Surefoot and Fleet Foot are strong early. The challenge is the missing arcane caster: no Haste, Stoneskin or Spell Sequencers, and only the Magpie's scrolls and wands (and, in ToB, Misdirection and Pocket Sand) for crowd control (with a fumble chance early on). Expect mage, lich and dragon fights in late SoA and Throne of Bhaal to need more preparation: stockpile scrolls through Brandobaris's Gift and Forger's Hand, keep the Slinger's Shots for protected casters, and let Still Ground and Hamstring blunt fights the party cannot simply out-cast.

The party starts strong, hits its hardest stretch in SoA mage fights, then comes into its own in Throne of Bhaal. Throughout, it is tough, mobile and well supplied, but has to solve magic problems without a mage.

| Stage | Strengths | Weaknesses | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BG1 (levels 1–8) | Fleet Foot and Slinger kill quickly. Surefoot cannot be feared, held or knocked back, and True Heart cannot be feared. Lay on Hands and Hearthsong keep everyone going. Magpie handles locks and traps and reads Sleep and Magic Missile scrolls. | Low HP all round. Poison from spiders and wyverns until the immunities arrive (True Heart at 5, Surefoot at 10). Early on, one in four Magpie scrolls fumbles (falling to one in five from level 5). | Easy |
| Early SoA (9–14) | Rapid Strike and grandmastery make the Fleet Foot a killer. Hearthsinger gets True Seeing (cleric level 9); True Heart gets Righteous Magic and charm and sleep immunity at 10. Surefoot shrugs off poison, paralysis and charm by 12. | Protected mages are the crunch point: Spell Thrust (3rd level) is available from the Magpie's scrolls at 7, but Breach (5th level) not until 13, and both with a fumble chance. Vampires drain levels and nobody is immune yet. | Hardest stretch |
| Late SoA (15–20) | Surefoot is nearly impossible to disable, with elemental resistance by 18–20. Magpie reads 6th-level scrolls from 16, the Slinger's damage keeps climbing, and the two-wide front line holds well. | Still no Haste, Stoneskin or Spell Sequencers. Healing is capped at 5th-level cleric spells (no Heal). Dragon breath and big area spells hit the lightly armoured Fleet Foot, Slinger and Magpie hard. | Moderate |
| Throne of Bhaal (21+) | Everything comes online: Breaching and Dispelling bullets crafted in town, Still Ground up to 3 times a day, the True Heart's healing chain up to Undying Heart, the Hearthsinger's innate Heal, Resurrection and other spell HLAs plus Hearth Guardians, the Fleet Foot's Time Slow, Time Pause and Hamstring, and the Magpie's 8th and 9th-level scrolls plus Forger's Hand. | No Time Stop spell (only the Fleet Foot's Time Slow and Time Pause), and few big summons beyond the True Heart's Deva and Planetar and the Hearthsinger's Hearth Guardians. Heavily layered ToB mages and liches still take careful sequencing. Undead and golems do not bleed. | Moderate, then comfortable |

**Consistent strengths:** excellent halfling saves against spells, wands and poison, with the Surefoot's and True Heart's immunities on top; a two-tank front line that rarely lets the back line get swarmed; the Fleet Foot's fast removal of enemy casters and archers; a self-supplying flow of scrolls, potions and gold from the Magpie; and full utility (locks, traps, scouting, True Seeing, Detect Illusion, Raise Dead, Turn Undead).

**Consistent weaknesses:** no arcane caster, so Haste, Stoneskin and Breach come only from scrolls or, for Breach in ToB, the Slinger's Shots, and big summons only from the True Heart's Deva and Planetar HLAs, the Hearthsinger's Hearth Guardians and scrolls; a healing ceiling in SoA (no Heal or Resurrection until ToB HLAs); halfling Strength topping out at 17, so the melee kits lean on Strength items; light armour on three of six (Fleet Foot, Slinger and Magpie), exposing them to area spells and breath; and no level-drain immunity until the Surefoot reaches 24 and the True Heart 30.

**Crunch points to plan for:**

1. Early SoA mages and liches: stockpile Breach, Dispel Magic and Spell Thrust scrolls for the Magpie, and use the Fleet Foot's speed to interrupt casting.
2. Vampires and level drain: carry Negative Plane Protection or restoration potions until the immunities arrive.
3. Late SoA dragons and big casters: keep resistance potions for the back line, and let the Surefoot and True Heart take the breath.

### Snow White and the Five Shorties

The best party the kits make: five halflings and one elf Sorcerer. Every all-halfling party is missing a real arcane caster, so the sixth slot goes to the one thing no kit can supply. It is the recommended six with the Slinger swapped for an elf Sorcerer.

| Position | Character | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arcane caster | Snow White: elf Sorcerer | Haste, Stoneskin, Breach, Spell Sequencers, Time Stop and arcane crowd control, cast again and again: everything a halfling party otherwise gets only from scrolls. Elves resist charm and sleep, which helps until Clear Heads arrives |
| Main tank | True Heart | Very hard to kill, a second source of healing, and its Aura of Courage covers the Sorcerer's weak saves; unlike the Surefoot, it doesn't stop Snow White casting |
| Bodyguard, anti-mage | Unbeguiled | Clear Heads protects the party's minds; Spoiling Strike Breaches enemy mages and, from 18, lowers their magic resistance for Snow White's spells; Unshakeable Wards stops enemies stripping her Stoneskin and Mantle; Stand Behind Me pulls hostile spells off her |
| Melee damage | Fleet Foot Fighter | The highest damage of all the kits, made higher by Snow White's Haste and Improved Haste; fast enough to reach enemy casters |
| Priest | Greenhand | Heal, Resurrection, Negative Plane Protection, True Seeing, and summons that Death Spell can't kill to keep enemies off Snow White |
| Thief | Magpie | Locks, traps and scouting. Brandobaris's Gift and Forger's Hand keep a stock of arcane scrolls that either of them can cast, covering the one-off spells Snow White doesn't know |

**Why it's the best:** the all-halfling parties answer enemy magic with workarounds; this one answers it head-on. Snow White brings the spells the halflings have only on scrolls, and the halflings cover her weaknesses: the True Heart and Unbeguiled keep enemies and hostile spells off her, Clear Heads keeps her mind her own, and the Magpie's scrolls fill the gaps in her short list of known spells.

**How it plays:** the True Heart and Unbeguiled hold the front with the Greenhand's summons around them, and Snow White stands behind them. She opens big fights with buffs and area spells and takes down enemy protections; the Unbeguiled spoils the casters that close in and the Fleet Foot, hasted, finishes them. The Greenhand heals and fills the field; the Magpie scouts ahead and covers the situational spells (Remove Curse, Dispel Magic variants, Protection from Undead and the like) from its scroll stock.

**Splitting the spells:** a Sorcerer knows only a few spells at each level, so pick the ones the party casts every fight: Haste and Improved Haste, Breach, Stoneskin, Mantle, Spell Sequencers, the main area damage and disables, and Time Stop. Leave anything used once a dungeon to the Magpie's scrolls.

**Watch out for:**
- **Summon limit:** Snow White and the Greenhand share the limit of 5. Let the Greenhand summon (its creatures resist Death Spell) and keep Snow White's picks for buffs, disables and damage.
- **The Fleet Foot with a Sorcerer behind it:** Improved Haste and Time Stop stack with Rapid Strike, Time Slow and Time Pause, and a Sorcerer can cast them more often than a Mage. The Fleet Foot is already on the playtest checklist for killing bosses before they act; this party makes that more likely.
- **Snow White is fragile:** low HP, no armour, and the first target for enemy archers and mages. Keep her behind the front line and protected by Stoneskin.
- **Don't swap in the Surefoot:** Still Ground stops Snow White casting too.
- **Check in game:** that elves can be Sorcerers in the Enhanced Editions (expected: human, elf and half-elf).

**Choice of caster: Sorcerer.** More casts per day than any Mage, chosen on the spot with no memorising, so it never runs out of the spells that win fights. The Magpie removes the Sorcerer's usual weakness, a short spell list, by supplying scrolls for everything else. And because a Sorcerer isn't a summon specialist, the Greenhand keeps the summon limit to itself.

- **Alternative, Conjurer (single-class Mage):** gets each new spell level one character level sooner up to 5th-level spells, so BG1 and early SoA are smoother, and can learn every spell, including the Magpie's finds and Forger's Hand copies. Its barred school, Divination, is already covered (True Seeing from the Greenhand, True Sight from the Unbeguiled, Identify from the Magpie's Lore). It casts fewer spells a day, and its specialty competes with the Greenhand for the summon limit.
- **Not a Fighter/Mage:** tougher, but casts high-level spells much later, and the party already has three fighters.

**Alternatives:** the Hearthsinger in place of the Greenhand for songs, plate and Kin's Shield, at the cost of Heal and Resurrection before ToB and the Death Spell-proof summons that guard Snow White. Worth it mainly if Snow White is a Conjurer who wants the summon limit for herself. The Nettle in place of the Magpie works better here than in any all-halfling party, because Snow White covers the arcane basics, but a Sorcerer then loses the scroll supply that covers her short spell list.

**Difficulty: easier than standard.** The easiest line-up in this document: the halfling parties' one shared weakness, the missing arcane caster, is gone.

| Stage | Strengths | Weaknesses | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BG1 (levels 1–8) | Sleep, Magic Missile and Web cast several times a day, not from fumbling scrolls. Fleet Foot kills quickly. The Greenhand's rabbits and the True Heart's plate keep enemies off Snow White. | Snow White has very low HP and reaches each spell level a character level later than a Mage. Poison until the True Heart's immunity at 5. | Easy |
| Early SoA (9–14) | Haste, Breach and Stoneskin cast without fumbles, and more often than a Mage could. Clear Heads from 8, Spoiling Strike Breaches from 12. Negative Plane Protection covers vampires. | Mind flayers and vampires target Snow White. Clear Heads covers only charm until 16. Few spells known, so lean on the Magpie's scrolls for anything unusual. | Moderate |
| Late SoA (15–20) | Spell Sequencers, Improved Haste, Mantle, Chain Contingency. Heal and Resurrection from the Greenhand. Spoiling Strike lowers enemy magic resistance from 18. | Dragon breath and area spells hit Snow White, the Fleet Foot and the Magpie hard. | Comfortable |
| Throne of Bhaal (21+) | Time Stop, 9th-level spells and Snow White's own HLAs, on top of the Unbeguiled's Unshakeable Wards and Stand Behind Me, the Fleet Foot's Time Pause and the Magpie's Forger's Hand copies. | Liches and dragons still need planning, but now with real answers. | Comfortable |

## Four-person parties

A smaller halfling party works well, and it has a hidden advantage: experience from kills is split four ways instead of six, so everyone levels noticeably faster. Immunities, higher scroll levels and HLAs all arrive sooner, which softens the early SoA crunch.

### Classic four

| Position | Kit | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front line | True Heart | Tank and healer in one: plate, Heart immunities, Lay on Hands, Cleansing Touch and the Heart healing chain |
| Front line | Hearthsinger | In heavy armour beside the True Heart; Hearthsong covers 30 feet, plus Dispel Magic, True Seeing and Raise Dead |
| Back line | Slinger | Steady ranged damage that interrupts enemy casters; Out of Reach knocks back anything that rushes the back line; Breaching and Dispelling Shot in Throne of Bhaal |
| Back line | Magpie | Locks, traps, scouting, Detect Illusion, and arcane scrolls and wands |

**How it plays:** a classic two-in-front, two-behind formation. The True Heart and Hearthsinger hold and heal, the Slinger interrupts and picks off casters from range, and the Magpie scouts, handles traps and fills magical gaps with scrolls. The Fleet Foot can replace the Slinger for faster, burstier kills, but it roams rather than holding the line, leaving the back line more exposed and losing the Slinger's ToB answer to protected mages.

**Difficulty: about the same as a six-person party, in a different shape.** Easier early and mid-game thanks to faster levelling; harder in big fights against many enemies, where only two front-liners hold and the back line can get caught. Against enemy mages in SoA the party still leans heavily on the Magpie's scrolls and the Hearthsinger's Dispel Magic until the Slinger's Shots arrive in ToB.

### Quintessential halfling four

Not the strongest line-up, but balanced and halfling all the way: a lucky fighter, a stubborn one, a garden priest and a light-fingered thief.

| Position | Kit | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front line | Tymora's Champion | Holds the front on luck: Lucky Dodge AC, save bonuses and Second Chance |
| Front line | Unbeguiled | Guards the party's minds with Clear Heads and spoils enemy casters with Spoiling Strike |
| Back line | Greenhand | Heals, summons a wider front line, scouts with its rabbit familiar and Clairvoyance, and brings the party's area damage |
| Back line | Magpie | Locks, traps, loot, and wands and scrolls in place of a mage |

**How it plays:** the two fighters hold, with the Greenhand's summons and Summon Horde rabbits soaking blows around them. The Unbeguiled shuts down enemy casters up close while the Greenhand's area spells thin out crowds, and the Magpie opens the way and covers arcane gaps with scrolls.

**Why it works:** Clear Heads covers the Greenhand's missing Chaotic Commands, the summons make a four-person front feel wider, and the Greenhand's Negative Plane Protection covers level drain.

**Gaps:** one healer (if the Greenhand goes down, the party has only potions, Harvest Loaf and any berries it is carrying); little ranged damage beyond the Greenhand's sling; and arcane magic only as good as the Magpie's stock.

**Difficulty: moderate.** Strong against mind control and protected casters for a party of four; weakest when the Greenhand is focused down.

### Snow White and the Three Shorties

The four-person version: the same elf Sorcerer with three halflings. Experience is split four ways, so Snow White reaches Spell Sequencers, Time Stop and her HLAs sooner than in the six.

| Position | Character | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arcane caster | Snow White: elf Sorcerer | The party's damage, crowd control and answer to enemy mages: Haste, Breach, Stoneskin, area spells and, later, Time Stop |
| Front line | True Heart | Tank and healer in one: plate, Heart immunities, Lay on Hands, and Aura of Courage over Snow White's saves. Cleansing Touch frees allies from hold and sleep from 10, and charm and confusion from 15 |
| Priest | Greenhand | Heal, Resurrection, Negative Plane Protection and True Seeing, and summons that widen a one-person front line around Snow White |
| Thief | Magpie | Locks, traps and scouting, plus the scroll stock that covers Snow White's short spell list |

**Why these three:** each covers something Snow White can't. The True Heart is the one body in plate between her and the enemy; the Greenhand gives the healing, raising and level-drain protection a Sorcerer has none of; the Magpie handles locks and traps and widens her spell list. The Unbeguiled and Fleet Foot drop out: in a four, the party needs healing and a thief more than mind protection or melee damage, and Snow White now does most of the killing.

**How it plays:** the True Heart holds the front with the Greenhand's summons around it, and Snow White and the Magpie stand behind. Snow White opens with buffs and area spells, Breaches protected mages and finishes what the summons pin down. The True Heart and Greenhand share the healing; the Magpie scouts ahead and fills gaps with scrolls.

**Weak points:**
- **Mind control:** no Clear Heads. Snow White resists charm as an elf, and the True Heart is immune from 10, but the Greenhand and Magpie are exposed. The True Heart's Cleansing Touch only frees charmed or confused allies from 15, so carry Remove Fear and anti-charm potions and scrolls before then.
- **A thin front line:** one front-liner, so the summons must hold the line. BG1, before the animal summons arrive at level 7, is the shakiest stretch: lean on Summon Horde and Snow White's Sleep and Web.
- **Summon limit:** Snow White and the Greenhand share the limit of 5, so as in the six, let the Greenhand summon.
- **Snow White is the main damage:** if she is disabled or killed mid-fight, the party loses most of its offence. Keep her protected by Stoneskin and Mirror Image and keep Resurrection ready.

**Alternatives:** the Unbeguiled in place of the True Heart for Clear Heads, Spoiling Strike and Stand Behind Me, at the cost of plate and the second healer; only the Greenhand then heals. The Fleet Foot in place of the Greenhand for much faster kills, at the cost of Heal, Resurrection and the summons; only for confident players who will rest often.

**Difficulty: easier than standard.** The faster levelling and a real arcane caster outweigh the thin front line, though BG1 and the first SoA mind flayers need care.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Early SoA (9–14) | Haste, Breach and Stoneskin. Heal from 11 and Negative Plane Protection. Insect Plague and Iron Skins. | Mind flayers and charm: no Clear Heads, and Cleansing Touch doesn't remove charm until 15. | Moderate |
| Late SoA (15–20) | Spell Sequencers, Improved Haste and Time Stop arriving early thanks to the four-way split. Resurrection, Creeping Doom and elementals. Cleansing Touch frees charmed and confused allies. | Dragon breath and area spells hit three of the four hard. | Comfortable |
| Throne of Bhaal (21+) | Snow White's HLAs, the Greenhand's Hedge Warden and Storm of Vengeance, the True Heart's Undying Heart and Deva and Planetar HLAs, and the Magpie's Forger's Hand copies. | Liches still need careful sequencing with only one caster to dispel and Breach them. | Comfortable |

## Three-person party

The smallest party that skips no content: it still has a thief, so every lock and trap gets handled. Experience is split three ways, so everyone levels much faster than in a party of six.

| Position | Kit | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front line | Unbeguiled | Immune to mind control early; Clear Heads protects the others' minds; Spoiling Strike Breaches protected mages; magic resistance soaks hostile spells |
| Back line | Greenhand | Healer, summoner and area damage: summons widen the front line, plus Insect Plague, Creeping Doom, Negative Plane Protection and True Seeing |
| Back line | Magpie | Every lock and trap, scouting, the party's gold and gear, and arcane scrolls and wands in place of a mage |

**How it plays:** the Unbeguiled holds the front with the Greenhand's summons around it, spoiling any enemy caster that comes close. The Greenhand heals, summons and opens big fights with area spells; the Magpie scouts ahead, clears the way and fills arcane gaps with scrolls.

**Why it works:** each kit covers the others' gaps. The Unbeguiled has no healing, the Greenhand has no mind protection or armour, and the Magpie has neither, but together they cover healing, mind control, protected mages, illusions, level drain, locks and traps. The summons take the hits a fourth or fifth party member would.

**Weak points:** only one real front-liner, and it can't wear magic rings or cloaks, so the summons must hold the line (BG1, before the animal summons arrive at level 7, is the shakiest stretch: lean on Summon Horde); only one healer, so protect the Greenhand and keep its berries in the others' packs; and little burst damage, so fights are won by area spells and summons rather than quick kills.

**Alternatives:** the Fleet Foot in place of the Unbeguiled for much faster kills, at the cost of the party's mind protection (carry potions or scrolls against charm); or the True Heart in place of the Unbeguiled for a second healer and plate, at the cost of Clear Heads. Keep the Magpie rather than the Nettle in any small party: the Nettle has no backstab damage and no scrolls.

**Difficulty: moderate.** Harder than four or six early on, when the front line is thin; easier than its size suggests from mid-SoA, once the summons and area spells arrive.

## Duo

A robust tank and a thief. Healing can always be covered by potions and resting, but this pair doesn't need to: the True Heart's healing comes free with the tank. Experience is split only two ways, so both level very fast.

| Position | Kit | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front line | True Heart | The best tank for a duo: plate, Heart immunities (poison at 5, charm and sleep at 10), Lay on Hands and the Heart healing chain; Deva and Planetar HLAs add extra bodies in ToB. Unlike the Surefoot, it doesn't stop the Magpie reading scrolls with Still Ground |
| Back line | Magpie | Every lock and trap, scouting, and arcane scrolls and wands in place of a mage |

**How it plays:** the True Heart holds the front and keeps both of them standing; the Magpie scouts ahead, clears the way and fights from behind with scrolls, wands and missiles. BG1 is the easiest start of any duo, with a plate-armoured tank from level 1.

**Weak points:** low damage (True Heart 5.5, Magpie 7), so fights take time and crowds grind on; no area spells or summons until the ToB HLAs, beyond the Magpie's scrolls and wands; enemy mages depend on the Magpie's stock of Breach, Dispel Magic and Spell Thrust scrolls, which can fumble early; and the Magpie is fragile and has no mind protection if enemies get past the True Heart.

**Alternative:** the Greenhand in place of the True Heart. It hits much harder from mid-SoA (area spells, summons as the front line) but has no real front line in BG1 until the animal summons arrive at level 7, so it needs more care early.

**Difficulty: moderate.** Easy in BG1, slower and harder against crowds and layered mages later; the faster levelling softens both.

## Swaps

- **Hearthsinger for Greenhand:** songs, plate and Homeward Hymn's extra attack instead of summons, area spells and scouting.
- **Surefoot for Unbeguiled:** a tougher front line, at the cost of the party's mind protection and Spoiling Strike. Keep the priests clear of Still Ground.
- **Unbeguiled for Surefoot or True Heart:** covers the original six's weak spots against magic.
- **Tymora's Champion for True Heart:** a durable second tank, at the cost of thinner healing, cleansing and raising.
- **Nettle for Magpie:** trades loot, scrolls and wands for traps and backstabs that take apart protected casters. Best when the party already has another answer to locks and to arcane gaps.
